# Architecture as riding terrain

These references informed the new structures. The game adapts their spatial ideas to downhill skating and snowboarding; the models are original, simplified game assets.

- [Oslo Opera House — Snøhetta](https://snohetta.prezly.com/the-norwegian-national-opera-and-ballet-159954): a publicly accessible roofscape of horizontal and sloping surfaces connects the building with its surroundings. Adaptation: low pavilions with a broad roof approach, an elevated terrace and a return slope. Glazed side bays give the roof a building underneath it.
- [StreetDome — CEBRA](https://www.cebra.dk/works/streetdome): the exterior joins a continuous skate landscape, including banks, stairs and slopes around the structure. Adaptation: open banked channels along ground routes, with curved sides for carving and transfers and eased ends that preserve forward travel.

Roof exits add a separate game-specific variation: occasional curved kickers sit at the edge, with a flat bypass beside them. The landing route is reserved across subsequent chunks. Jump height comes from the visible shape and normal player physics.

The main road remains continuous. Structures are optional side routes, use shared materials and static chunk collision, and work in both riding modes. No direction arrows, required checkpoints, per-structure lights or artificial movement forces are used.

Potential later additions include terraced forecourts with grindable retaining edges, loading-yard transfers between adjacent roofs, and covered ground routes with an accessible upper deck. Each needs a complete approach, riding surface, bypass and landing before it becomes part of generation.
